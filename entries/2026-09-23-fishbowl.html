Interaction Field Study Report at Fishbowl Computing Center

September 23, 2026 · Week 4

Subject

What we studied: How students complete a printing task using the mPrint system - from entering the lab to picking up a finished print.

Where we studied: Fishbowl Computing Lab, University of Michigan

Date observed: 09/21/2026

What we tried to understand: The full sequence of decisions and steps a student goes through to print a document in order to find interaction points worth redesigning.

  • Which printing method they choose
  • How they navigate the MPrint website/app
  • How they retrieve their printout

Evidence & Experience Map

The printing process includes 8 phases, from entering the lab to picking up the finished print. I went through the printing process myself using the MPrint website path (used on a laptop) to print my assignment (colored) as our example route through the system.

  1. Enter the Fishbowl: enter the Fishbowl Computing Lab to print
  2. Choose the way to print: there is three ways to print (MPrint website, MPrint mobile app, or swipe an M-Card at the printer)
  3. Use the MPrint website
  4. Select the destination: type/search a destination (e.g., “fishbowl”) to choose and browse the printer and features
  5. Upload a file from local or cloud drive
  6. Choose options: set numbers of copies, orientation, pages per sheet, size, page range, and duplex options.
  7. Submit a job request: it will appear in the session print history, where status and queuing can be checked
  8. Pick up an order: retrieve the print once status is “ready”; the lobby screen also shows status and uniquename

Observations

The printing process includes 8 phases, from entering the lab to picking up the finished print. I went through the printing process myself using the MPrint website path (used on a laptop) to print my assignment (colored) as our example route through the system.

1. Multiple printing spaces

There are three separate printing spaces (Computing Site 1, Computing Site 2, and Multi) in the lab, located far away from each other. Student appears to choose whichever is closest to their desk or best matches their need (e.g., going to Multi for color or tabloid) rather than defaulting to one station.

2. Students rely on habit more than in-the-moment guidance

Although MPrint offers three ways to print, student observed appeared to go straight to their own usual method, suggesting the choice is driven by prior experience rather than posted instructions.

Click to expand images

3. Minimal Wayfinding at the entrance

There is very little signage or wayfinding cue at the entrance pointing toward the printing stations. The status screen showing printer availability is mounted far from the entrance, and the closer secondary entrance only has Computing Site 2 in view. This could potentially be confusing for students navigating the printing station for their first time. Especially for those printing color or tabloid, as the Multi is far from either entrance and there’s no signage towards it.

The printing/info desk is visible from both entrances, and could provide guidance for printing. There’s no observation of pick-up at the info desk, even though pickup is a noteworthy activity as it goes through another pathway to print non-standard or large size posters.

4. Detailed guide exist, but only help once you’re already there

Instructional posters are posted at two of the three stations and are quite detailed, but they only become useful after a student has already found the right station. They don’t solve the initial wayfinding problem.
